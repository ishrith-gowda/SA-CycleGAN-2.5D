Image translation for harmonization and domain adaptation is routinely selected on distributional fidelity metrics such as FID, on the assumption that a more target-like image is more useful: a team can lower FID and ship a model that is worse at the task. That fidelity can mis-rank generators is known; we make a sharper, mechanistic claim: the utility loss is a property of the learned distribution-matching objective itself, not of any architecture, which is why it survives the move from GANs to diffusion. Our instrument is a frozen downstream evaluator scored alongside FID on identical inputs, plus a matched non-learned control. On GTA5-to-Cityscapes (N=1000, frozen SegFormer), CycleGAN more than halves FID (137.2 to 65.5) while mIoU falls 0.380 to 0.167; the non-learned control preserves mIoU (0.363) without improving FID. Sweeping SDEdit's noise strength traces a monotonic frontier: FID 145.1 to 88.0 as mIoU falls 0.323 to 0.106, with seed standard deviation at most 0.005 mIoU. Two controls close the obvious escapes: with an empty prompt, fidelity is worse than the untranslated source (FID 154.2) yet mIoU still collapses (0.170), decoupling the damage from any fidelity gain; and edge-conditioned ControlNet does not escape the frontier (mIoU 0.221 at FID 133.2, versus 0.289 at matched FID). Multi-site brain-tumor MRI reproduces it: frozen nnU-Net tumor Dice falls 0.682 to 0.064 under learned harmonization but holds at 0.669 non-learned. Across both generative families and both domains, the low-FID/high-utility corner stayed empty. We do not claim fidelity is uninformative, only that it is non-monotone in utility once a learned prior enters the loop; our contribution is a measurement and a protocol, not a method.
